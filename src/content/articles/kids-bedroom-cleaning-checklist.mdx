“Clean your room” sends one child straight to building with the toys they were supposed to put away. Another stands in the doorway, unsure where to begin.

**Follow one small path: trash, laundry, toys, bed, floor. Show each stop, give things reachable homes, and let your child check the finished step before moving on.**

<FamilyPrintable resource="bedroom" />

## Set up the room before handing over the list

Put a hamper where clothes come off, give toys bins your child can open, and leave room on a low shelf for books. Keep ordinary trash easy to reach. An adult handles cleaning products, high places and unsafe objects.

The five stops are a tidy-up. Vacuuming, washing bedding and cleaning surfaces belong on a separate household plan, with an adult providing suitable help.

For a broader approach to [sharing responsibilities as a family](/resources/intentional-family-life), start with the jobs each person can learn. The room does not need to match a magazine photo.

## Walk through the first reset together

Print the page at actual size on US Letter paper. Type your child's name and the starting cue into the PDF first, or write them after printing. Hang it where they can see it.

Point to the first stop and complete it together. Ask your child to find ordinary paper scraps, put them in the bin, and check the box. Continue only as far as they can manage with your help.

For the next reset, let them lead a familiar step while you stay nearby. A preschooler might practice only toys and bed; another child may be ready for the whole path. Use [chores by age and readiness](/resources/chores-for-kids-by-age) as a starting menu.

## Give unfinished play a real parking spot

A puzzle or building project can stay in one agreed place that leaves the walking path clear. Decide that spot before cleanup begins.

Keep the same rule for everyday toys: each category has a home. If the bins are overflowing, pause and sort them with an adult rather than expecting a child to solve the storage problem.

Use [the “clean means this” guide](/resources/kids-cleaning-checklist) when you need to agree on what an individual finished job looks like.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When bedtime is already close">

**Only five minutes?** Put dirty clothes in the hamper, toys in bins and clear the walking path. Stop at the timer.

**The room is far beyond a reset?** An adult helps clear one category at a time. Return to the short checklist afterward.

**A step still needs help?** Stay with that step. Checking a box is not proof that the skill has been learned.

</WhenLifeHappens>
