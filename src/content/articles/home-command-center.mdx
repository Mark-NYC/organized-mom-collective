It’s 7:45. Someone needs a signed permission slip, the keys aren’t on the hook, and the school flyer is somewhere under yesterday’s mail. A prettier organizer won’t help if papers still stop in three different places.

Here’s the short answer: **put one calendar, two paper pockets and a pen where your family already passes. Give papers a way in and a way back out.** A home command center can fit on the side of the fridge or a small patch of wall.

It’s the physical spot for [your family’s weekly planning system](/resources/how-to-organize-your-familys-week).

<Shift title="Give every paper an exit.">

A basket catches the permission slip. It doesn’t get it signed or back into the backpack. **Separate papers that need attention from papers ready to leave.** Your command center earns its space when it helps the next thing happen, rather than keeping the pile tidy.

</Shift>

<RealLifeVersion title="Set up the smallest useful command center">

1. **Choose the spot before buying anything.** Use the kitchen wall, the side of the fridge or the entry your family actually uses. Keep it clear of the door’s swing and within reach of the people who handle the papers.
2. **Put the calendar there, with a pen.** If the schedule is digital, keep using it. This spot can just handle paper. For a paper calendar, [use entries everyone can read](/resources/how-to-organize-a-family-calendar).
3. **Label two pockets “Needs attention” and “Ready to go.”** Folders or clips you already own work. A form moves from the first to the second once it’s signed, then into the right backpack before bedtime.
4. **Keep keys and bags nearby.** Use an existing hook or bowl. Backpacks can sit below or beside the station; they don’t need to hang on the same wall.
5. **Check the pockets after dinner.** Agree on who checks them tonight. Add dates to the calendar, handle anything due tomorrow, and move finished papers into bags. Start with 2 minutes; make time separately for a form that needs longer.

During [the weekly family reset](/resources/weekly-family-reset), check these pockets for dates and forms that affect the coming week.

**Leave off anything that belongs elsewhere:** old schoolwork, keepsakes, bills you’re filing and extra charts nobody reads. Copy the date from a flyer, then recycle it if you no longer need its details. Keep papers with private information out of public view.

</RealLifeVersion>

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the pockets turn into piles">

**Behind this week? Pull out only what’s due next.** Handle tomorrow’s form before sorting last month’s papers.

**No wall space? Use two folders in a tray.** Keep the pen there and bags nearby. You can test the setup without hanging anything.

**Nobody uses it? Move it to where papers actually land.** Walk one form through the process together before adding another organizer.

</WhenLifeHappens>
