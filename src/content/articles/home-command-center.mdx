It’s 7:45. Someone needs a signed permission slip, the keys aren’t on the hook, and the school flyer is under yesterday’s mail.

**Put one calendar, two paper pockets and a pen where your family already passes. Give papers a way in and a way back out, with someone responsible for moving them.** It fits on the side of the fridge or a small patch of wall.

<Shift title="Give every paper an exit.">

A basket catches the permission slip. It doesn’t get it signed or back into the backpack. **Separate papers that need attention from papers ready to leave.** Your command center earns its space when it helps the next thing happen, rather than keeping the pile tidy.

</Shift>

## Hand over the school-paper check

Children can learn to put incoming papers in the first pocket and returned forms in their bags. **The rest of the job can belong to one adult, start to finish.**

1. **Agree on the whole job.** The owner checks incoming papers, notes deadlines, gets forms signed and returns them to the right bag.
2. **Walk one form through together.** Show the two pockets, where dates go and which papers need an adult. Children can learn where to put papers after school.
3. **Let the owner keep it moving.** They choose their reminder and bring upcoming deadlines to the weekly check-in. You don’t need to inspect the pockets each night.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the pockets turn into piles">

**Behind this week? Pull out only what’s due next.** Handle tomorrow’s form before sorting last month’s papers.

**No wall space? Use two folders in a tray.** Keep the pen there and bags nearby. You can test the setup without hanging anything.

**Nobody uses it? Move it to where papers actually land.** Walk one form through the process together before adding another organizer. Still not helping after a few weeks? [Track what keeps coming back to you](/resources/where-does-my-time-go) and fix those first.

</WhenLifeHappens>
