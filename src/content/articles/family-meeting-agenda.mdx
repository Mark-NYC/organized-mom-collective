Everyone has ideas about what needs to change at home. Finding a time to hear them without turning it into a long meeting is the hard part.

**Use a ten-minute family meeting with four parts: Our Focus (2 minutes), Let’s Talk (3), Everyone’s Part (3), and Let’s Practice (2).** Then stop. It's the same meeting rhythm used in the Organized Family Club.

**[Print the free one-page family meeting agenda](/family-meeting-agenda).** It has space for your focus, what you heard, the next action and who owns it. You can print another copy next month.

<Shift title="A family meeting isn't another planning job for one adult.">

A good meeting doesn't end with one person holding a new list of reminders. **Let each person name their part and practice it before the conversation ends.** A child might own putting their backpack away; an adult still owns adult decisions and tasks.

</Shift>

## What this sounds like at home

Imagine the focus is *easier school mornings*. Someone mentions that backpacks keep disappearing. The family decides that each child puts their own bag by the door after school. Everyone tries the walk from the door to the hook together. That's a decision, an owner and a first practice in ten minutes.

Looking for a quick cleanup before bed? Use the [10-minute family tidy-up](/resources/ten-minute-family-reset), where everyone puts things away. This meeting is for talking, agreeing, and practicing one shared change.

Want to sort rides, meals and appointments instead? Use the separate [weekly family reset](/resources/weekly-family-reset). Need to transfer a recurring household job? Read [how to share the mental load](/resources/how-to-share-the-mental-load).

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the meeting gets messy">

**Someone won't talk?** Let them point, draw or pass. Don't force a contribution.

**Only five minutes?** Pick the focus, hear one idea and agree on one next step. Practice later.

**The plan didn't stick?** Keep the same four parts next month. Choose a smaller job to try.

</WhenLifeHappens>
