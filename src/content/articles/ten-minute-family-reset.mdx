Dinner is over, toys are underfoot, and tomorrow’s school things are scattered. Nobody needs a long list of chores right before bed.

**For a 10-minute family reset, set one timer and share four jobs: clear the table, reset the floor, ready the kitchen, and prep tomorrow.** This is a hands-on evening tidy-up. For a conversation about a family focus and everyone’s part, use the separate [10-minute family meeting agenda](/resources/family-meeting-agenda).

Choose the owners before starting. These are **parallel jobs within one ten-minute window**, not ten minutes per job. With fewer people, take the most useful jobs first and leave the rest when time runs out.

## Keep the finish line small

The table is usable, the walking path is clear, dishes have reached the kitchen, and tomorrow’s essentials have a home. That’s enough.

Leave sorting drawers, scrubbing pans, and deciding the whole week’s schedule for another time. The [weekly family reset](/resources/weekly-family-reset) is for rides, meals, and appointments. This routine is for putting things back tonight.

## Give each person a job they can finish

A child can return their own toys to a reachable bin. Another person can clear papers from the table. An adult takes dishes and unfamiliar school requirements. Choose jobs your children have already practiced; adults handle sharp objects and cleaning products.

Keep the same job for a few evenings so everyone knows where to begin. The cue might be “after dinner,” with a timer anyone can start. For guidance on choosing tasks, see [chores for kids by age](/resources/chores-for-kids-by-age).

<WhenLifeHappens title="When ten minutes is too much">

**Only three minutes?** Clear the walking path and gather the things that need to leave tomorrow.

**One person is home?** Choose one area. Four jobs are options to share, not four demands on one adult.

**Missed last night?** Start tonight’s timer. Don’t double it to catch up.

</WhenLifeHappens>
