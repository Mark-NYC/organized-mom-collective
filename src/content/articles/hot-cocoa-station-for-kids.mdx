A hot cocoa station looks lovely until somebody spills marshmallows and leaves three sticky mugs on the counter.

**Give the station four clear jobs: set out mugs, measure cocoa, let an adult handle hot liquid, and reset the tray afterward.** Kids can own the safe parts.

## What belongs on the station

Start with a small tray or the corner of a counter: mugs, cocoa mix, a measuring spoon, napkins and one or two toppings. Keep extra supplies in a nearby cupboard.

<Tip label="Safety first" title="Keep hot water with an adult.">Boiling water, kettles and hot milk can burn. Adults should heat and pour for younger children. Check drink temperature before serving, and avoid small hard toppings for children who could choke.</Tip>

## A pretty setup that doesn't create extra work

The trick is to keep the pieces your family actually uses, not build an elaborate display.

<SplitList left={{label:'Easy to repeat',items:['One tray','One or two toppings','A place for used mugs']}} right={{label:'Not required',items:['A special cart','A dozen glass jars','A decorated sign for every ingredient']}} />

A child can be the **Cocoa Captain** for the week: check the napkins, return the toppings and tell an adult when the mix is low. That is one job with a clear finish.

For other simple kitchen handoffs, read [kids helping with dinner](/resources/kids-helping-with-dinner) and [teaching kids independence](/resources/teaching-kids-independence).

## Try a cocoa-night rhythm

Set the tray out for a Friday movie night or a winter afternoon. Let each person prepare the safe parts of their own mug, then clear their dishes when finished.

The tradition works even when it's just ordinary mugs and a packet of cocoa. There is no need to turn it into a production.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the afternoon gets busy">

**No time for the full station?** Set out mugs and cocoa packets. That is enough.

**Forgot to restock?** Make it a shared reset next time instead of adding another reminder to Mom's list.

</WhenLifeHappens>
