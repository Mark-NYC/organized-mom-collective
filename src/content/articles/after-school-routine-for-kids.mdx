The door opens, the backpack drops, and a lunchbox lands wherever there's room. By dinner, there's a school form to sign and nobody knows where the water bottle went.

**Start with one after-school drop zone: a hook for each child's bag, one “Needs attention” pocket for papers, and a place to unload lunchboxes and gear. Then teach a short arrival routine kids can finish themselves.**

<Shift title="The landing spot isn't the whole routine.">

A bench catches a backpack, but it can't empty a lunchbox or get a permission slip signed. **The win is a short sequence with a clear finish:** everything has landed where it belongs, hands are washed, and your child can move on to snack. No adult has to keep calling out the next step.

</Shift>

## Set up the after-school drop zone

You don't need a new organizer. Use the bench, hooks or small stretch of wall near the door your children actually use.

- **Bags:** Give each child one reachable hook. A labeled floor spot works if hooks aren't possible.
- **Papers:** Put every notice and form in the **“Needs attention”** pocket from your [home command center](/resources/home-command-center). Don't create a second paper pile.
- **Little extras:** Take lunchboxes to the sink, empty water bottles, put shoes on the mat and return jackets or sports gear to their homes.

This is the [morning launch pad](/resources/launch-pad-by-the-door) in reverse: **same spot, opposite direction.** Afternoon is for unloading; evening is for preparing what leaves tomorrow.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When afternoons don't go to plan">

**Straight from pickup to practice?** Bring the lunchbox in when you finally get home. Do the arrival steps then, not twice.

**A pile instead of a spot?** Remove what doesn't belong there and practice the first two steps again. Don't buy another bin yet.

**Tiny entryway?** Use one bag hook per child and a narrow shoe mat. Keep the paper pocket on the kitchen wall if that's where it gets checked.

</WhenLifeHappens>
