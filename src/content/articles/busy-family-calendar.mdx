The coach moves practice to Thursday. Your child's school announces early dismissal. A dentist reminder comes in while you're making dinner. The calendar exists, but the changes are still sitting in three different places.

**To manage a busy family calendar, collect changes in one shared place, write down the person responsible, and check tomorrow's plan together for five minutes.**

<Tip label="Before you start" title="One rule for schedule updates.">

Whoever receives a change updates the calendar or directly asks the person who owns that activity to do it. **Forwarding a message is not the same as confirming the plan changed.**

</Tip>

## Make one change visible to everyone

Here is what a useful entry looks like:

> **Thu 4:30 · Soccer practice · Dad drives · cleats + water**  
> *Changed from Tuesday. Confirmed with the coach.*

If the pickup has changed, update the pickup entry too. That is the detail another adult needs to act on.

The calendar should answer *where, when and who*. Keep forms and paperwork at your [home command center](/resources/home-command-center). For the initial setup, use [the family calendar guide](/resources/how-to-organize-a-family-calendar).

## Share the responsibility

An adult can own updates from a particular school, coach or activity. A younger child can check tomorrow's plan with you and put their gear near the door. An older child can help spot a changed practice time, with an adult confirming the details.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the plan changes again">

**A text arrives at the last minute?** Update the shared calendar and tell the person whose ride or preparation changes. Don't assume they'll notice it in time.

**Missed the evening check?** Review the next appointment and pickup first. Start again tomorrow.

**Two events overlap?** Decide which commitment stays and who handles the other. Write the decision on the calendar so no one has to ask twice.

</WhenLifeHappens>
