The show ends, but the tablet stays in someone's hands. Dinner is ready, and now you're negotiating for the tenth time about when screen time is over.

**Give screen time a clear place in the day: agree when it starts, what is allowed, how it ends, where devices go, and what happens next. Practice the routine together.**

<Tip label="Before you start" title="Make the rules together.">

Ask your child which transition feels hardest and practice turning off a screen when nobody is upset. The [American Academy of Pediatrics family media plan](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/How-to-Make-a-Family-Media-Use-Plan.aspx) recommends family-specific rules, screen-free times and spaces, and room for offline activities.

</Tip>

## Try this after-school example

> **Snack and play → agreed screen time → device on shelf → help set the table → dinner.**

This is an example, not a required schedule. A different family may use screen time after dinner or not every day. The important part is that everyone knows the ending and next step before the device starts.

## Keep the boundary simple

  left={{ label: 'Make it clear', items: ['One agreed start and stop cue', 'Age-appropriate content', 'A named place for the device', 'A visible next activity'] }}

A rule like "the tablet goes on the shelf when the timer ends" is easier to practice than "don't spend too long on screens." Adults can model the same device-free dinner habit.

## Let kids help own the routine

Younger children can carry the device to its shelf with you. Children who read can follow a short picture-and-word sequence. Adults still choose content, manage settings, and decide the overall limits. If your child needs a visible reminder, see the [free morning routine chart generator](/resources/morning-routine-chart-generator) for ideas about making steps easy to follow; it is not a dedicated screen time tool.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the transition doesn't go smoothly">

**They protest at the end?** Stay with the agreed boundary, acknowledge the frustration and help them move to the next activity. Practice the stop cue again on a calmer day.

**Plans change?** Explain the exception before screen time begins. Keep the device parking spot and end cue the same.

**The routine keeps falling apart?** Simplify it to one clear start cue and one clear end cue. Review the fit during your [weekly family reset](/resources/weekly-family-reset).

</WhenLifeHappens>
