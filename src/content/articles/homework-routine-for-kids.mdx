The backpack is open, the worksheet is somewhere underneath the lunchbox, and you've already asked three times whether homework has started. More reminders won't fix a routine that nobody can see.

**A homework routine kids can follow starts with a clear transition, a predictable starting cue, one manageable work block and a place to put finished work.** Keep the steps short enough for your child to check without you narrating the whole afternoon.

## A simple after-school homework routine

<Timeline id="homework-plan" kicker="The real-life version" title="Four steps kids can practice" items={[
  { time: 'First', title: 'Unpack and take a breather', body: 'Put lunch items away, get water or a snack, and agree on a short transition before starting.' },
  { time: 'Next', title: 'Check what needs doing', body: 'Open the school folder or assignment list together. Pick one clear first task.' },
  { time: 'Then', title: 'Work, with help available', body: 'Start the task. Use a short work block or a movement break if that helps your child focus.' },
  { time: 'Last', title: 'Check and pack away', body: 'Review what is finished, flag questions for the teacher, and return materials to the backpack or agreed spot.' },
]} />

<Tip label="Keep the goal realistic" title="A routine supports homework. It can't make the work easy.">

Some children need more adult help, breaks, a quieter space or school accommodations. Keep assignments and expectations aligned with their teacher. Never turn the chart into a timer for punishment.

</Tip>

## Adjust the plan to your child's afternoon

<SplitList
  left={{ label: 'Keep', items: ['A start time or consistent cue', 'One visible first task', 'Breaks that match your child', 'A clear pack-up finish'] }}
  right={{ label: 'Skip', items: ['A rigid schedule that clashes with activities', 'Every assignment shown as one huge job', 'Expecting the chart to replace needed help'] }}
/>

You can make homework the first activity after snack, or allow outdoor play before starting. The best order is the one your family can practice consistently. If your child comes home exhausted, try a longer transition and talk with the teacher when the workload routinely exceeds what they can manage.

The arrival steps belong in our [after-school drop-zone routine](/resources/after-school-routine-for-kids). For practicing the job without repeating every instruction, see [how to teach kids independence](/resources/teaching-kids-independence).

<WhenLifeHappens title="When homework doesn't go to plan">

**They don't know what was assigned?** Check the school's usual source together, and set up one reliable way to collect assignments.

**They're stuck and frustrated?** Help name the hard part, take a break, and contact the teacher if the same problem keeps happening.

**You're out the door for an activity?** Move the routine to a workable window instead of squeezing a full assignment into ten frantic minutes.

</WhenLifeHappens>
