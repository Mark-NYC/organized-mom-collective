It's 5:45, practice ran late, and dinner is still a decision. The hardest part may not be cooking. It's deciding what fits tonight.

**Plan three types of dinner: a full meal, a quick meal and a minimal-effort meal. Match each to your family's schedule before you write the grocery list.**

<ReferenceChart kicker="Save this dinner framework" title="Full, quick or minimal effort" columns={['Kind of night','Dinner that fits']} rows={[['Full night','A meal with some prep: sheet-pan chicken, roasted vegetables and rice'],['Quick night','Fifteen-minute pasta, quesadillas or egg sandwiches'],['Minimal-effort night','Leftovers, freezer food or a simple pantry meal']]}>These are examples, not rules. Repeat meals your family already likes.</ReferenceChart>

<Tip label="Before you start" title="Look at the calendar before the recipes.">Mark late practices, meetings and appointments. Give the hardest nights the easiest meals. A beautiful menu nobody has time to cook isn't a plan.</Tip>

## Build the week in one sitting

Start with the two busiest evenings and assign them minimal-effort meals. Give the quieter nights full or quick dinners. Plan one leftover night if you typically have enough food, then check pantry, refrigerator and freezer before shopping.

Make the grocery list directly from the chosen dinners. Put ingredients for two meals in the same shopping category so you're not making multiple trips through the store.

## Let everyone take a part

Kids can help set the table, wash appropriate produce with supervision and clear their place. Older kids can take a recurring dinner-prep step they've learned. Another adult can own one entire dinner, including checking ingredients and cooking it.

For more ideas, read [kids helping with dinner](/resources/kids-helping-with-dinner).

<WhenLifeHappens title="When dinner plans fall apart">

**Practice runs late?** Swap the full meal for your minimal-effort option.

**Missing an ingredient?** Choose a pantry backup instead of another store run.

**Nobody wants the planned meal?** Use a familiar family favorite and move on.

</WhenLifeHappens>
