Thursday’s square says “Dentist.” It doesn’t say what time, who’s driving, or that soccer is the same night.

## Should a family use a weekly or monthly wall calendar?

**It depends on how much happens each day.** A monthly calendar is enough if your weeks are fairly steady and you mostly need to see dates coming: birthdays, school breaks, trips. A weekly one works better when each day has rides, practices and dinners to sort out, because a month square holds a line or two. Busy family? Use both: the month for dates, the week for the plan, copied over once a week.

## One week, two calendars

Take a typical school week, for example: school every day, soccer Tuesday and Thursday, piano Wednesday, and a dentist visit Thursday. Here’s Thursday in each format:

| On a month square | On a weekly page |
| --- | --- |
| Dentist | 3:30 Dentist · Mia · Dad drives · insurance card |
| Soccer | 5:30 Soccer · Sam · cleats, water |
| | Dinner: tacos, 15 minutes |
| | To-do: sign the field trip form |
| | Cleaning: Kitchen Reset |

**The month tells you Thursday is busy. The week tells everyone what to do about it.** That’s why the week is where [a family calendar everyone actually uses](/resources/how-to-organize-a-family-calendar) does its work, and the month is where you spot the birthday party three weeks out.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When your format isn’t working">

**The month is full, but nobody knows the plan?** Keep the month for dates and add one weekly page for this week only.

**The weekly page is stale by Wednesday?** Fill it in once, at a set time each week, not as things come up.

**Running both feels like double work?** Copy only this week’s dated items from the month onto the week, once, on the same day each week.

</WhenLifeHappens>
