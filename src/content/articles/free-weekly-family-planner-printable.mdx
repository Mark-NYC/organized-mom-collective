It’s Sunday evening. The school emails are scattered, someone has practice on Tuesday, and dinner still needs a plan. You know most of what’s happening, but everyone else keeps asking you.

**Start with a single weekly family planner: seven days, meals, groceries and to-dos on one printable page.** Fill in our [free weekly family planner](/planner) on your phone or computer, download the PNG and print it for the fridge or family command center. No purchase required.

<Shift title="The goal isn’t a prettier to-do list. It’s a shared plan.">

A planner only in Mom’s head still leaves Mom answering every question. A plan everyone can see makes the week easier to share. **Add who owns the pickup, dinner or task, not just when it happens.** The person responsible can check the page without needing another reminder.

</Shift>

<RealLifeVersion title="Fill in your family's week in 10 minutes">

1. **Put the fixed events in first (3 minutes).** Add school deadlines, appointments, activities, pickups and who is driving. Write “Soccer, 5 pm, Dad drives” rather than just “Soccer.”
2. **Choose the dinners (3 minutes).** Look at the late nights before filling in the family menu. Use leftovers or sandwiches on the nights with no cooking time.
3. **Make one grocery list (2 minutes).** Check what you have, then add only what you need for the meals you picked.
4. **Share the week’s work (2 minutes).** Put two or three specific jobs in the to-do area with a name beside each. Let family members own a whole job, not wait for instructions.
5. **Print and place it somewhere visible.** The refrigerator or family command center works. Spend two minutes together checking the plan so everyone knows where to look.

</RealLifeVersion>

<WhenLifeHappens title="When your week changes">

**A practice gets canceled?** Cross it out. Write the change on the copy everyone uses.

**The week is already packed?** Fill in the appointments, dinner shortcuts and one important to-do. Leave the rest blank.

**You miss Sunday planning?** Start Wednesday. A useful half-week beats waiting for a perfect Monday.

</WhenLifeHappens>
