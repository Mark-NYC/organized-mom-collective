It’s Wednesday night. The counters are covered, the schedule on the fridge says “bathrooms,” and you haven’t done Tuesday’s yet.

Most cleaning schedules fall apart right here, because they were written for a week where nothing goes wrong. This one is built for the other kind: **a 15-minute reset every day, one small zone Monday to Thursday, and a weekend you can use or skip.**

## The weekly cleaning schedule

Monday to Thursday takes 25 or 30 minutes, in two short blocks.

<CleaningWeek print />

<Shift title="A schedule’s real job is to tell you what to skip.">

Most schedules assume every task gets done every day, so one missed day leaves you two behind. A schedule that works decides two things for you: **what to clean today, and what to let go when today doesn’t cooperate.**

</Shift>

## The Daily Reset

Some mess grows overnight: dishes, crumbs, shoes, clothes on the floor. Fifteen minutes a day stops it stacking up. Same five things, nothing to decide:

Here’s [the 15-minute Daily Reset, step by step](/resources/daily-cleaning-routine), with the order and a five-minute version for hard days.

One area per weekday, always the same one. Do the list, or set a timer, and stop when either runs out. If Wednesday is your longest day, swap it for a smaller zone.

Thursday’s fridge check is also the easiest moment to [plan next week’s dinners](/resources/weekly-meal-planning-for-families): you’ve just seen what needs using up.

**Friday to Sunday are optional.** Do one task from the month’s project (“{monthlyDeepClean[9].title}” in October), catch up on one missed zone, or take the weekend off.

<WhenLifeHappens title="What happens when you miss a day">

One rule: **skip it, don’t stack it.** If Tuesday’s bedrooms didn’t happen, Wednesday is still Entry/Bathroom. Two zones in one night is how ten minutes becomes an hour.
