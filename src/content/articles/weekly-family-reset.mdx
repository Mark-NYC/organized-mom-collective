Monday’s pickup is covered. Thursday’s isn’t. Dinner plans are still scattered across three messages. Someone needs to put the coming week in one place.

**Use a 15-minute weekly schedule reset to update the calendar, settle rides, choose dinners, name an owner for loose ends and protect one family priority.** This is a practical logistics check for the people handling those details, not a second family meeting.

For a conversation with the whole family about what you're working on together, use the separate **[10-minute Family Meeting](/resources/family-meeting-agenda)**. That follows the same four parts every month: Our Focus, Let’s Talk, Everyone’s Part and Let’s Practice.

This check keeps [your shared calendar](/resources/how-to-organize-a-family-calendar) accurate. Sunday works if that's convenient, but any regular time before the busy days is fine.

<Tip label="Before you start" title="Bring the calendar and the people who handle the details.">

Bring school messages, pickup updates and whoever has information or owns decisions for the coming week. The whole family doesn't need to sit through logistics. Let children add dates or flag a form when useful. If dinner planning needs longer, use the [meal-planning method](/resources/weekly-meal-planning-for-families) separately.

</Tip>

<Shift title="Make the decisions. Leave the jobs for later.">

“Plan the meals” can turn into cooking them. “Check the school papers” can turn into sorting every drawer. **Keep the reset for decisions that affect the week.** Write down the job and who will handle it, then keep going. The person taking groceries can shop after the timer ends.

</Shift>

## Share the planning, not another meeting

<ArticleIconSequence ariaLabel="Weekly schedule check: check dates, decide rides and meals, assign owners, write down the plan" items={[{label:'Check the week',icon:'svg/calendar'},{label:'Make decisions',icon:'svg/conversation-bubbles'},{label:'Name owners',icon:'svg/family-group'},{label:'Write it down',icon:'svg/clipboard-with-checklist'}]} caption="This weekly check is for logistics. The jobs happen afterward." />


1. **Split up the information.** Each person responsible for school messages, activities or groceries brings the changes they handle.
2. **Make the calendar readable.** Put the actual pickup person beside the time. Give any unresolved task a name and a deadline.
3. **Let the owner remember.** Whoever takes the pickup, form or grocery job owns its follow-through, including reminders. No one needs to chair a formal meeting to do this.

<WhenLifeHappens title="The five-minute version for a rough week">

**Only five minutes?** Check tomorrow and the next two days. Confirm the rides, decide those dinners and handle any form due tomorrow. Leave the rest for another check.

**Missed Sunday?** Do that smaller version tonight. Don’t wait a week to restart.

**Nobody else is available?** Update what you know, then ask the relevant person to settle their part. The calendar should not become one person's permanent reminder list.

</WhenLifeHappens>

## Two different habits, two different jobs

- **[Monthly Family Meeting](/resources/family-meeting-agenda):** the whole family follows four parts in ten minutes to hear each other, agree on responsibilities and practice one thing. This is the family conversation, not this weekly calendar check.
- **[Free weekly planner](/planner):** put the dates, pickups and dinners on one printable page.
- **[Morning routine chart](/resources/morning-routine-chart-generator):** give kids a visible sequence for school mornings instead of repeating reminders.
