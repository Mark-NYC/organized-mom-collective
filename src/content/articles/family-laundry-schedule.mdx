The washer ran on Tuesday. On Thursday, the clean clothes are still in a basket on the couch, and someone is asking where their soccer socks are.

**A family laundry schedule gives each load a regular day and one owner who takes it all the way through four stages: wash, dry, fold and put away.** The load isn’t done until it’s in the drawer.

Two or three laundry days are enough for most families. Choose days that are already quieter, not the night of three practices.

<Tip label="Before you start" title="One basket per person for clean clothes.">

When the dryer stops, sort straight into each person’s basket. Each basket goes to its own room, so folding and putting away can be someone else’s job, not one big pile on the couch.

</Tip>

## The one rule: finish the load you start

Most laundry piles aren’t a washing problem. They’re a folding and putting-away problem. **Start a load only when someone can take it all the way to the drawer that day.**

## Laundry jobs kids can take

You don’t have to run every stage. **Give each child one stage they own, every laundry day.**

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the week gets away from you">

**Missed laundry day? Run the one load people need.** Usually school clothes or towels. The rest waits for its next day.

**Clean pile already on the couch?** Sort it into each person’s basket now and let everyone put their own away before dinner.

**Sick week?** Bedding and towels first. Folding can wait; putting away still counts.

</WhenLifeHappens>
