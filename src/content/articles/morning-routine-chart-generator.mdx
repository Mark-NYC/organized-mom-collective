## Example: five steps before school

Here’s an illustrative school morning, leaving at 8:00. Change the times to fit your own school run.

| Time | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 7:10 | Get dressed |
| 7:20 | Eat breakfast |
| 7:40 | Brush teeth |
| 7:50 | Put shoes on |
| 7:55 | Grab backpack |

<Tip label="Good to know" title="A printed list doesn’t teach the jobs by itself.">

Practice the steps together before expecting independent follow-through. Read the chart with a child who needs help, show where things belong and ask “What’s next on your chart?” Let them try before stepping in.

</Tip>

## Teach the routine before stepping back

1. **Make the chart together.** Let your child choose the colors and name the steps they already know. Keep only what belongs in the morning.
2. **Practice finding the next step.** Read together if needed, then let them do the job. Show where shoes and school things live.
3. **Share adult support.** Agree on which caregiver handles the school-morning check. As your child learns, use fewer spoken prompts and let them check their own chart.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the morning goes sideways">

**Late start? Help with the essential steps** and leave optional jobs for later. Don’t turn a missed morning into catch-up work tomorrow.

**Shoes or the school folder keep holding things up?** Practice returning them to one landing spot the night before. Agree on which adult handles school papers, rather than leaving that check to you by default.

**Two steps keep getting mixed up?** Change their order on the chart rather than repeating a routine that doesn’t fit.

</WhenLifeHappens>
