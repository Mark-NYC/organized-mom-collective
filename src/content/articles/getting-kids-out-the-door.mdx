The shoes are missing. A water bottle is empty. Someone remembers a signed form two minutes before leaving. The morning is full of decisions nobody wants to make under pressure.

**Prepare one door-side launch spot the night before and repeat the same five checks each morning. The goal is fewer decisions, not faster children.**

<Tip label="Before you start" title="Use the door you actually leave through.">A hook for each backpack, one shoe spot and a basket for small essentials work even in a narrow entryway. Don't build a showroom mudroom to solve a morning problem.</Tip>

## Give the checklist to the kids

Do the five checks together for several mornings. Then point to the routine rather than naming each job again. Ask “What's next on your list?” Younger kids may need pictures; older kids can check their own backpacks and activity bags.

## Practice when nobody is late

On a relaxed afternoon, have everyone try the exit routine. Watch where things get stuck: a crowded shoe bin, missing hooks or two children needing the bathroom together. Fix the bottleneck instead of adding reminders.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the morning goes sideways">

**Overslept?** Skip optional extras. Bring the essentials and go.

**Can't find something?** Check the launch spot first, then rebuild it later.

**Checklist is ignored?** Remove mastered steps and keep the two still being forgotten.

</WhenLifeHappens>
