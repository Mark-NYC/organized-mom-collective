It's faster to pack the backpack yourself. But tomorrow, you'll be packing it again. How do you help your child learn to handle the little things without turning every morning into a reminder marathon?

**To teach kids independence, choose one safe, age-appropriate job, show what finished looks like, practice together, then step back while your child learns to check and complete it.**

  ]}
/>

<Tip label="Start small" title="Practice when you're not rushing.">

Teach the backpack routine after school or before bed, not while everyone is trying to leave. Keep sharp tools, household cleaners, hot surfaces and other unsafe jobs with adults.

</Tip>

## What independence looks like in everyday life

<SplitList
  left={{ label: 'Give them room', items: ['A clear, reachable place for supplies', 'One job with a visible finish line', 'Time to try, make a mistake and correct it'] }}
  right={{ label: 'Skip for now', items: ['Five new jobs at once', 'Redoing the work secretly', 'Expecting a new habit without practice'] }}
/>

A child who can put their shoes away but forgets when to do it hasn't necessarily mastered the whole routine. **Learning to notice the job is part of learning to own it.**

## Try a backpack handoff this week

<Timeline
  kicker="One week, one job"
  items={[

  ]}

This is a practice sequence, not a deadline. Different children will need different amounts of repetition.

For more options matched to your child's abilities, use our [chores-for-kids-by-age guide](/resources/chores-for-kids-by-age). For an even smaller first job, try [hanging a towel and putting away a toothbrush cup](/resources/bathroom-chores-for-kids).

<WhenLifeHappens title="When your child still needs reminders">

**They forget again?** Walk them back to the picture cue and practice the same job. Don't add three more instructions.

**The job is too hard?** Make it smaller. “Put your lunchbox on the counter” can come before “unpack your whole bag.”

**You're in a rush?** Help today without declaring the experiment over. Practice again when there's room.

</WhenLifeHappens>
