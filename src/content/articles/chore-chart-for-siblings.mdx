## Fair does not have to mean identical

The printable shows an illustrative dinner cleanup: a younger child sets napkins and spoons, another clears unbreakable dishes, and an older child wipes the table. The adults handle the remaining work. These are examples, not age requirements.

Choose from [jobs children can practice at different ages](/resources/chores-for-kids-by-age), then adjust for your child's abilities. A familiar job may take one child two minutes and another much longer. Notice that difference before adding more.

This is one way to make [shared family responsibility](/resources/intentional-family-life) concrete. Adults have a named part too.

## Make a plan for your actual family

Download the PDF. Type into its name and job fields in a PDF reader, or write them after printing. Leave unused person rows blank.

Start with one shared moment, such as dinner cleanup. Agree on a job, a cue and a visible finish for each person. Practice together before expecting the chart to carry the reminder.

At a short weekly check-in, ask each person whether their job still fits. A child who finishes quickly can keep that job; finishing does not automatically earn them everyone else's work.

For a larger household, use the [Family Responsibility Builder](/family-responsibility-builder) to make a chart with parents and children on the same page.

For a shared Saturday reset, try [weekend chores for kids with a clear stopping point](/resources/weekend-chores-for-kids).

## Rotate a job only when both people know it

Two children who can both set the table could swap weeks. Put the next swap date on the plan and change the names together.

Keep a job steady while someone is learning it. An older sibling does not need to teach, supervise or check the younger child's work. That support belongs to an adult.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the job split starts an argument">

**“They have less than me.”** Compare the actual effort and help needed. Adjust a job that has grown too large.

**Someone is sick or away?** Agree on a smaller plan for tonight. Return to the usual jobs when they can join again.

**The chart is ignored?** Practice the starting cue together. Adding more rows will not teach the first job.

</WhenLifeHappens>
