The dentist is in your phone. The practice change is in a text from the coach. The field trip form is still in a backpack. Nobody else knows any of it, so everyone asks you.

Here’s the short answer: **use one calendar, hang it where everyone walks past it, write every entry the same way, and look at the week before it starts.**

It’s the first piece of [a simple system for the family’s week](/resources/how-to-organize-your-familys-week).

<Shift title="A family calendar is for reading, not just writing.">

Most advice is about the format: paper or app, which colors, which categories. But a calendar only one person keeps is just a record. **The test is simple: can anyone in the house read tomorrow without asking you?**

</Shift>

<RealLifeVersion title="How to set up a family calendar">

1. **Pick one calendar.** Paper or app both work. Two calendars means two half-right ones.
2. **Hang it where everyone walks past it.** By the fridge or the door you leave by, at kid eye level, with a pen beside it.
3. **Write every entry the same way.** Time, what, who, and what to bring. For example:

   **3:30 · Soccer · Sam, Dad drives · cleats, water**

<Figure
  image="fp-02-week-entries"
  alt="Monday to Friday on a filled-in page of the wall calendar: each day starts with 8:00 school drop-off, then 3:00 pick-up and snack, practices and homework, then dinner at 5:30 or 6:00, with each day’s cleaning zone at the bottom of its column."
  caption="Times first, one line each, the same shape every day."
/>

4. **Put on it what changes the day.** Leave off what never changes:

| On the calendar | Off the calendar |
| --- | --- |
| School runs, pick-ups, rides | Every-day bedtimes and routines |
| Practices, lessons, appointments | Long to-do lists |
| Birthdays, parties, forms due | Notes that need a paragraph |
| Who’s home late, dinner on busy nights | Anything nobody needs to act on |

5. **Colors are optional.** If you use them, one per person, and black for the whole family.
6. **Look at the week before it starts.** Five minutes on Sunday: who drives where, which nights are short, what needs packing. The short nights are the ones to [plan easy dinners for](/resources/weekly-meal-planning-for-families).

Whoever adds an event writes it the same way. Kids who can read check it themselves instead of asking.

Each day’s cleaning zone can live at the bottom of its column too, so the [weekly cleaning schedule](/resources/weekly-cleaning-schedule-for-busy-moms) is where everyone can see it.

</RealLifeVersion>

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the calendar falls behind">

**Missed the Sunday look? Just look at tomorrow tonight.** Do the full week next weekend.

**Plans change midweek? Cross it out and write the new time beside it,** so everyone sees it changed.

**Off the calendar for weeks?** Start with this week. Don’t fill in what already happened.

</WhenLifeHappens>
