It’s 5 pm. Someone asks what’s for dinner, and you open the fridge hoping it will decide for you. It doesn’t, so you decide. Again.

Here’s the short answer: **once a week, look at the week ahead, label each night by how much time you’ll actually have, plan four or five dinners to match, and write the grocery list from that plan.** It takes about 20 minutes.

<Shift title="Plan the nights before the food.">

Most meal plans start with recipes and quietly assume seven nights of cooking. Then Tuesday has practice until 6:30, and the plan is over by Wednesday. The problem isn’t ideas. **Sort the nights first, then choose dinners that fit them.**

</Shift>

<RealLifeVersion title="The 20-minute meal planning method">

1. **Look at the week.** Open the calendar and note every late pickup, practice, meeting and night out.
2. **Label each night.** Full, short or zero, by the time someone will actually be home to cook:

<Versions
  title="Three kinds of night"
  items={[
    { name: 'Full night', when: 'home early, nothing on', body: 'Cook a real recipe, and make enough for another night.', time: '45+ min' },
    { name: 'Short night', when: 'practice, late pickup', body: 'A dinner you can make without a recipe: tacos, pasta, eggs and toast.', time: '20 min' },
    { name: 'Zero night', when: 'out, late, or done', body: 'Leftovers, something from the freezer, sandwiches or takeout.', time: '0–5 min' },
  ]}
/>

3. **Plan four or five dinners.** Full nights get the recipes; short nights get your quick defaults. Zero nights need no plan beyond “leftovers” or “takeout”, and that counts.
4. **Shop the kitchen first.** Check the fridge and pantry, then write the grocery list straight from the plan.
5. **Put it where everyone sees it.** Fridge door or family calendar, so “what’s for dinner?” has an answer nobody has to ask you for.

Here’s what a week can look like:

| Night | Kind | Dinner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Short | Tacos |
| Tue | Full | Roast chicken (make double) |
| Wed | Zero | Chicken wraps |
| Thu | Short | Pasta and salad |
| Fri | Zero | Takeout |

Notice Tuesday’s chicken is doing two jobs: it’s Wednesday’s dinner too.

</RealLifeVersion>

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the week doesn’t follow the plan">

**Swap, don’t scrap.** If Tuesday turns into a zero night, move Tuesday’s dinner to the next night that has time and push the rest along.

**Missed your planning day?** Plan only the next three nights. A short plan beats deciding at 5 pm.

**On a hard week, every night can be a zero night.** Sandwiches and freezer dinners, written down, are still a plan.

</WhenLifeHappens>
