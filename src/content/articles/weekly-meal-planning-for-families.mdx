It’s 5 pm and someone asks what’s for dinner. You decide. Again.

Here’s the short answer: **once a week, look at the nights together, choose four or five dinners that fit, and agree on who handles each meal and the grocery list.** About 20 minutes.

It’s the dinner part of [planning the family’s week once, on Sunday](/resources/how-to-organize-your-familys-week).

<Shift title="Plan the nights before the food.">

Most meal plans start with recipes and assume seven nights of cooking. One late practice and the plan is over. **Sort the nights first, then pick dinners that fit them.**

</Shift>

<RealLifeVersion title="The 20-minute meal planning method">

1. **Look at the week together.** Note every late pickup, practice and night out from [the family calendar](/resources/how-to-organize-a-family-calendar).

2. **Label each night** full, short or zero:

3. **Plan four or five dinners to fit, with an owner for each.** That person checks supplies and handles the meal, including the reminders. Zero nights count as planned.

## Hand over a dinner from start to finish

1. **Agree on a night and the whole job.** Choosing a meal, checking supplies, preparing it and deciding what happens if plans change.
2. **Share the information once.** Show where the plan, groceries and family food requirements live. Let the adult taking dinner choose their method.
3. **Teach children a repeatable part.** Setting the table or gathering lunch items, with support that fits their age. The dinner owner checks readiness instead of waiting for reminders from you.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the week changes">

**Swap, don’t scrap.** The person handling that dinner can move it to the next suitable night and update the plan. Agree on a swap if responsibility needs to change.

**Hard week?** Every night can be a zero night. Agree on who checks the freezer or arranges the simple dinner; it still doesn’t need to fall to you. Written down, it’s still a plan, and it leaves [less to clean after dinner](/resources/daily-cleaning-routine).
