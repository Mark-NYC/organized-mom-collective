It’s 5 pm and someone asks what’s for dinner. You decide. Again.

Here’s the short answer: **once a week, label each night by how much time you’ll have, plan four or five dinners to match, and write the grocery list from that plan.** About 20 minutes.

It’s the dinner part of [planning the family’s week once, on Sunday](/resources/how-to-organize-your-familys-week).

<Shift title="Plan the nights before the food.">

Most meal plans start with recipes and assume seven nights of cooking. One late practice and the plan is over. **Sort the nights first, then pick dinners that fit them.**

</Shift>

<RealLifeVersion title="The 20-minute meal planning method">

1. **Look at the week together.** Note every late pickup, practice and night out from [the family calendar](/resources/how-to-organize-a-family-calendar).

<Figure
  image="fp-05-week"
  alt="A filled-in week on the wall calendar beside a coffee mug and a pen, with dinners written into the days: chicken tacos on Tuesday, pizza night on Wednesday, leftovers on Thursday."
  caption="Dinners written into the week: tacos, pizza night, leftovers."
/>

2. **Label each night** full, short or zero:

<Versions
  title="Three kinds of night"
  items={[
    { name: 'Full night', body: 'Cook a real recipe, and make enough for another night.', time: '45+ min' },
    { name: 'Short night', body: 'A dinner you can make without a recipe: tacos, pasta, eggs and toast.', time: '20 min' },
    { name: 'Zero night', body: 'Leftovers, something from the freezer, sandwiches or takeout.', time: '0–5 min' },
  ]}
/>

<Figure
  image="fp-05-taco-skillet"
  alt="A hand stirring taco meat in a cast-iron skillet with a wooden spoon, next to a stack of tortillas, shredded cheese and cut limes on the counter."
  caption="A short night: tacos, no recipe needed."
/>

3. **Plan four or five dinners to fit, with an owner for each.** That person checks supplies and handles the meal, including the reminders. Zero nights count as planned.

<Figure
  image="fp-05-meal-plan"
  alt="Close-up of the wall calendar’s meal plan and grocery list: meals written for each day of the week, from turkey wraps and chicken tacos on Monday to slow-cook chili on Sunday, and a grocery list with milk, eggs, tortillas and chicken breasts."
  caption="The week’s meals and the grocery list, side by side."
/>

4. **Agree on who owns groceries,** including checking the fridge, writing the list and shopping. Thursday’s [fridge check](/resources/weekly-cleaning-schedule-for-busy-moms#the-real-life-version) is a good moment. The [free weekly dinner planner](/meal-planner) has the three kinds of night and the list on one page.

/>

</RealLifeVersion>

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the week changes">

**Swap, don’t scrap.** Move a missed dinner to the next night with time.

**Hard week?** Every night can be a zero night. Written down, it’s still a plan, and it leaves [less to clean after dinner](/resources/daily-cleaning-routine).
