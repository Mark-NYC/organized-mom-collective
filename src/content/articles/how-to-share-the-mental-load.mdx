Someone packs the lunches. You still check whether there’s bread, remind them about the early field trip and notice the lunchboxes need washing. The packing changed hands. The remembering didn’t.

Here’s the short answer: **agree on one whole job someone else will own, including noticing what’s needed, planning it and following through.** Write down what “done” means and try it for two weeks before adding another job.

Sharing household work makes room for [the family life you’re trying to build](/resources/intentional-family-life).

<Shift title="The reminder is part of the job.">

“Tell me what needs doing” still leaves you keeping track. **Include the noticing and remembering in the handoff.** A shared list helps someone see the work, but you haven’t handed it over if you’re still checking every deadline and asking whether it happened.

</Shift>

<RealLifeVersion title="Hand over one whole job">

1. **Pick a recurring job together.** School lunches, laundry or the school-paper pocket. Choose something the other adult agrees to take, with room in their week to do it.
2. **Describe the whole job together.** For lunches: check supplies, add missing food to the grocery list, pack, get the bags out the door and wash the containers. Include the school’s food rules and any allergy requirements.
3. **Agree on the finish line.** “Lunches packed and in the bags by 7:30” is clearer than “help with mornings.” Share where the supplies and information live. Walk through unfamiliar parts once.
4. **Move the reminders too.** The new owner sets their own alerts and checks the relevant messages. Keep shared dates and papers in [one visible household spot](/resources/home-command-center).
5. **Check in after two weeks.** Each person brings what worked, what got missed and what needs changing. Let them choose the method when it meets the agreed finish line; don’t redo it because it looks different from yours. The [free mental load worksheet](/mental-load-worksheet) walks through all five on one page.

| Whole job | What goes with it |
| --- | --- |
| School lunches | Supplies, packing, departure, containers |
| Laundry | Notice the pile, wash, dry, put away |
| School-paper pocket | Check forms, note deadlines, return them |

**Example: handing off school lunches for two weeks**

| Agree on this | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Dad owns the full school-lunch routine, not just packing. |
| Notice and plan | Check the school calendar and lunch supplies on Sunday; add missing items to the shared grocery list. |
| Do the job | Pack lunches, place them in backpacks by 7:30, and wash containers after school. |
| Remember | Dad sets his own reminder and checks for field-trip or early-dismissal changes. |
| Check in | After two weeks, both adults decide whether the handoff worked or needs adjusting. |

This is an **illustrative agreement**, not a rule for which parent should do lunches. Swap the job, owner and deadline to fit your family. Use the [free mental load worksheet](/mental-load-worksheet) to write down your own version.

</RealLifeVersion>

## Teach a child-sized version

1. **Choose a job they can learn.** Start with getting their backpack ready or putting clean clothes away, rather than an entire household process.
2. **Practice the check as well as the action.** Show where the list lives, what they need and what finished looks like. Do it together before expecting independence.
3. **Give them room to try.** Help with unfamiliar steps and check in at an agreed time. Share the teaching with other caregivers; you don’t have to become the only coach.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the handoff needs adjusting">

**Something got missed?** Agree on a fix and a reminder the owner can use. Don’t automatically take the whole job back.

**Someone’s week is overloaded?** Agree on a temporary swap, including when ownership returns. Review it during [the weekly family reset](/resources/weekly-family-reset).

**The other adult won’t take a job?** A better checklist won’t create agreement. Discuss what each adult is willing and able to own. More organizing on your part won’t replace that agreement.

</WhenLifeHappens>
