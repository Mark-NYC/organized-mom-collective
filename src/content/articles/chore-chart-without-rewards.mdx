**A chore chart without rewards works when everyday jobs are part of living together: three jobs, each tied to a moment, on a chart your child checks themselves, with you noticing the effort instead of paying for it.**

<Tip label="Before you start" title="Rewards aren’t wrong. They’re just optional.">

Some families pay for extra jobs or celebrate a good week, and that can work. This is for the everyday jobs: the ones you want done because the family needs them, not because there’s a prize.

</Tip>

## A no-rewards routine by age

Pick three jobs from your child’s age list and give each one a when. These come from our [chores by age guide](/resources/chores-for-kids-by-age).

| Age | Three jobs, each with a when |
| --- | --- |
| 4–5 | Put toys away before dinner · Put out napkins and spoons at dinner · Dirty clothes in the hamper at bath time |
| 6–8 | Make their bed after getting dressed · Set and clear the table at dinner · Put clean clothes in their drawers on laundry day |
| 9–12 | Fold and put away their own laundry on laundry day · Take out the trash and recycling on trash night · Strip and remake their bed once a week |

**Teach before you expect.** Do each job together a few times and agree on what done looks like.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When they push back">

**“What do I get?”** Answer once, calmly: “Nothing extra. It’s part of living here.” Then point to the chart.

**Skipped a job?** Start with today. Don’t make up yesterday’s box.

**Every job is a fight?** The job is probably too big or not taught yet. Shrink it and practice again.

</WhenLifeHappens>
