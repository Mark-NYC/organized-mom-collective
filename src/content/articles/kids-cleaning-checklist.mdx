“I already cleaned it.” You look at the floor. Your child looks at the shelf. You were picturing different things.

**Name the job and its visible finish: toys in their bins, clothes put away, dishes at the agreed spot, or a cover pulled up with the pillow back. Teach one finish line before adding another.**

<FamilyPrintable resource="finish-lines" />

## Show the finish before asking for it

Choose one row from the printable. Stand beside your child and point out where those things belong. Put the last two items away together, then look back at the space.

Ask, “What do you see when this job is finished?” Let them describe it or point. A child can learn the picture before reading every word.

For a personal reference, take a photo of that one finished shelf or play area and place it beside the chart. Keep it about the job, with no need to photograph your child.

Clear expectations are one piece of [helping everyone take part at home](/resources/intentional-family-life). The adult's part includes reachable storage and time to practice.

## Give each item somewhere it can actually go

A full toy bin leaves no room to finish “put toys away.” A drawer a child cannot open makes the job depend on an adult.

Before adding the job, clear enough space for its things and show where they go. Keep the finish small: the blocks are in the block bin, rather than the whole playroom looks perfect.

Match the task to [your child's practiced household skills](/resources/chores-for-kids-by-age). Keep hot, sharp, heavy or breakable items for an adult.

## Let your child do the last check

After practicing, point back to the row and ask your child to compare the space with its finish line. If something is missing, name that one part: “The books still need their shelf.”

The printable includes space for a name, today's job and a finish line of your own. Download it to type into those fields or write by hand.

This chart defines individual jobs. For a room-wide order, use the separate [five-stop bedroom reset](/resources/kids-bedroom-cleaning-checklist). For deciding who owns which job, use the [sibling chore chart](/resources/chore-chart-for-siblings).
