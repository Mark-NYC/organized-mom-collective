The coach moves practice by text at 2 pm. Your phone knows. The calendar on the fridge still says 4:00, and that's the one the kids read.

## Should you get a digital wall calendar or a paper one?

**It depends on where your changes come from.** A digital wall calendar display is the better buy if plans change several times a week, the adults already keep the schedule in phone calendars, and nobody wants to hand-write. A paper wall calendar is enough if your week is fairly steady and the real problem is that nobody looks. Want both? Keep appointments digital and write only this week's family plan on paper. If what you have works, don't switch.

## Digital display vs. paper at a glance

| What you need | Digital wall display | Paper wall calendar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Changes made away from home | Appear once someone edits a connected calendar | Wait until someone writes them in |
| Upfront cost | A device | The calendar |
| Ongoing cost | Optional plans for extra features | None |
| Kids reading tomorrow | Works if the screen stays simple | Works at their height |
| Adding things | Tap, type or edit on a phone | A pen |
| Meals and lists beside the schedule | Depends on the device and plan | On planners built for it |
| Power and Wi-Fi | Needs both | Neither |
| What goes stale | Anything that never reaches a calendar | Anything nobody wrote down |

<WhenLifeHappens title="When the wall and the phones disagree">

**Paper says 4:00, the display says 5:00?** The digital calendar wins on times. Fix the paper at the next weekly look.

**The display is current, but nobody checks it?** Look at tomorrow together after dinner, two minutes. The habit matters more than the hardware.

**Paper is stale every week by Wednesday?** Your week changes faster than a pen can keep up. That's a real reason to go digital.

</WhenLifeHappens>
