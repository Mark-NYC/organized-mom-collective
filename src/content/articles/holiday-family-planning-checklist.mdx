The school party, gift exchange and family dinner all arrive in the same week. Somehow one person ends up remembering the dates, the shopping and who's bringing what.

**Make four lists: dates, people, food and tasks. Assign one owner to every job, including getting supplies and following through.**

<Tip label="Before you start" title="You don't need a holiday binder.">Keep these lists on one sheet beside your regular family planner. The point is visibility and ownership, not prettier planning supplies.</Tip>

## Plan backward from the busiest date

Three or four weeks ahead, decide which gatherings matter and what your family won't take on. Two weeks ahead, assign shopping and prep. The week before, confirm food and rides. The day before, pack what you can and stop adding tasks.

Starting late? Skip the timeline. Make the four lists and choose the next urgent action.

## Give someone the whole responsibility

“Can you help with drinks?” still leaves you managing drinks. Instead say, “Can you choose, buy and bring the drinks for Saturday?” Older kids can wrap gifts, make place cards or set out games. Younger kids can help fold napkins or decorate.

<WhenLifeHappens title="When plans change">

**A guest cancels?** Update the food count and tell whoever owns that dish.

**Someone gets sick?** Keep the essentials and drop optional extras.

**Too many invitations?** Choose the gatherings that matter most and decline one.

</WhenLifeHappens>
