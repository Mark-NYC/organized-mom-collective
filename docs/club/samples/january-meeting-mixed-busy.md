# Our Family Meeting Companion
**January 2027 · The Bennett Family**

> Sample: 3 · Children of different ages with competing schedules (5, 9 and 13, two adults). Generated by `scripts/meeting-guide-samples.ts`.

Ideas for our January Family Meetings.

_Use the four parts from your Family Meeting guide. Pick what fits at each meeting, come back to what matters, skip the rest._

### 1. Our Focus
_What we’re working on together, and why it helps all of us._

- **Sharing activities and rides** · Everyone sees what’s coming, and the kids help get ready for their own activities.
- **Sharing the work** · The work at home is spread so no one carries most of it.

### 2. Let’s Talk
_Everyone answers, the youngest first. No wrong answers, no fixing anyone._

**Conversation starters**
- What’s happening for each of us this week, like piano?
- Which activity or ride catches us by surprise most?
- Which jobs at home could someone else try?
- What gets in our way? One possibility: plans might be living in one person’s head or phone.

_Little ones can point or answer with one word._

---

### 3. Everyone’s Part
_Each person picks or agrees to one small part they own this month._

_Ideas to choose from, not jobs. Decide together._

- **Mia** · 5: Coat on the hook, shoes in the shoe spot. **or** Set out the napkins and spoons. _(Do it together at first, then step back.)_
- **Leo** · 9: Clear and rinse the dishes after dinner. **or** Keep track of gear and water bottle. _(Ready for more independence.)_
- **Jade** · 13: Keep track of keys, chargers and gear. **or** Help cook one dinner a week. _(Free to choose how and when.)_ · Jade already takes care of laundry. Worth noticing out loud.
- For the grown-ups to talk through: how will we help without taking over, and how will we notice the effort?
- Marcus already takes care of driving and pickups. Worth noticing out loud.

### 4. Let’s Practice
_Try one part together right now, so the first time isn’t a reminder later._

**Ways to make it stick**
- If there’s time, try a part together before the meeting ends.
- One idea: hang the calendar where everyone passes, and add plans the day they’re made.
- It can help to tie a part to something that already happens. For Mia: “Coming in the door, coat on the hook, shoes in the shoe spot.”
- To make it easier for Mia: a low hook and a shoe spot by the door.

**Coming back to it:** At a later meeting, you might check how a part is going: on my own, with reminders, or hard. Keep it, shrink it or swap it.
