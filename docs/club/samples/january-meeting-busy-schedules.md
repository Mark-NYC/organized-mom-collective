**January 2027 · The Lindqvist Family**

> Sample: 4 · Busy family struggling with schedules and activities (11 and 15, two adults). Generated by `scripts/meeting-guide-samples.ts`.

Ideas for our January Family Meetings.

_Use the four parts from your Family Meeting guide. Pick what fits at each meeting, come back to what matters, skip the rest._

### 1. Our Focus
_What we’re working on together, and why it helps all of us._

- **Everyone knowing the plan** · Nobody is surprised by what’s happening this week, and the kids get ready for their own activities.

### 2. Let’s Talk
_Everyone answers, the youngest first. No wrong answers, no fixing anyone._

**Conversation starters**
- What’s happening for each of us this week, like robotics?
- What gets in the way? One possibility: plans might be living in one person’s head or phone.
- Ask Elsa what’s coming up that everyone should know.
- Ask Nils which things go missing most often.

---

### 3. Everyone’s Part
_Each person picks or agrees to one small part they own this month._

_Ideas to choose from, not jobs. Decide together._

- **Elsa** · 15: Keep plans on the family calendar, rides included, and ask for rides a day ahead. _(Free to choose how and when.)_
- **Nils** · 11: Coming home, put gear and water bottle in one home spot. _(Ready for more independence.)_
- For the grown-ups to talk through: how will we help without taking over, and how will we notice the effort?

### 4. Let’s Practice
_Try one part together right now, so the first time isn’t a reminder later._

**Ways to make it stick**
- One thing worth trying: hang the calendar where everyone passes, and add plans the day they’re made.
- When it’s going well, say so, and hold off on adding a new job.

**Coming back to it:** At a later meeting, you might check how it’s going: on my own, with reminders, or hard. Keep it, shrink it or swap it.
