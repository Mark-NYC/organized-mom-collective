# Our Family Meeting Companion
**January 2027 · The Lindqvist Family**

> Sample: Two adults with a teenager. Generated by `scripts/meeting-guide-samples.ts`.

Ideas for our January Family Meetings.

_Use the four parts from your Family Meeting guide. Pick what fits at each meeting, come back to what matters, skip the rest._

### 1. Our Focus
_What we’re working on together, and why it helps all of us._

- **Fewer reminders** · The kids remember their own parts, so one person isn’t everyone’s reminder.
- **Sharing plans with each other** · Everyone hears about plans before they happen.

### 2. Let’s Talk
_Everyone answers, the youngest first. No wrong answers, no fixing anyone._

**Conversation starters**
- What’s happening for each of us this week, like robotics?
- What could each of us remember on our own if we knew when to do it?
- How does each of us like to hear about plans?
- What gets in our way? One possibility: plans might be getting shared in passing.

---

### 3. Everyone’s Part
_Each person picks or agrees to one small part they own this month._

_Ideas to choose from, not jobs. Decide together._

- **Elsa** · 15: Set a school-night wind-down time and keep it. **or** Keep plans on the family calendar, rides included. _(Free to choose how and when.)_
- For the grown-ups to talk through: how will we help without taking over, and how will we notice the effort?

### 4. Let’s Practice
_Try one part together right now, so the first time isn’t a reminder later._

**Ways to make it stick**
- If there’s time, try a part together before the meeting ends.
- One idea: share new plans at a meeting and write them on the calendar that day.
- It can help to tie a part to something that already happens. For Elsa: “A phone alarm, set a school-night wind-down time and keep it.”
- If a part keeps slipping, try a smaller version before adding reminders.

**Coming back to it:** At a later meeting, you might check how a part is going: on my own, with reminders, or hard. Keep it, shrink it or swap it.
