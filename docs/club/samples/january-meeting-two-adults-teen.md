Ideas for this month’s meetings, made for our family. Use what fits, come back to what matters, skip the rest.

_Your Family Meeting guide explains the four parts. Bring this page to any meeting in January and pick an idea or two from each part._

### 1. Our Focus
_What we’re working on together, and why it helps all of us._

- **Fewer reminders** · The kids remember their own parts, so one person isn’t everyone’s reminder.
- **Sharing plans with each other** · Everyone hears about plans before they happen.

### 2. Let’s Talk
_Everyone answers, the youngest first. No wrong answers, no fixing anyone._

**Conversation starters**
- What’s happening for each of us this week, like robotics?
- What could each of us remember on our own if we knew when to do it?
- How does each of us like to hear about plans?
- What gets in our way? One possibility: plans might be getting shared in passing.

---

### 3. Everyone’s Part
_Each person picks or agrees to one small part they own this month._

_Ideas to choose from, not jobs. Decide together; one small part each is plenty._

- **Elsa** · 15: Sets their own wind-down time on school nights and keeps it. **or** Keeps their own plans on the family calendar, rides included. _(Their way, their call.)_
- Maja and Sam: add your own plans to the calendar, and leave the kids’ plans to them.

### 4. Let’s Practice
_Try one part together right now, so the first time isn’t a reminder later._

**Ways to make it stick**
- Try a part once, right now, while everyone’s together.
- New plans get said out loud at the meeting and written on the calendar that day.
- Give each part a clear “when” that follows something already done: “Their own phone alarm, Elsa sets their own wind-down time on school nights and keeps it.”
- If a part keeps slipping, make it smaller instead of reminding more.

**Coming back to it:** At a later meeting, come back to a part: on my own, with reminders, or hard? Keep it, shrink it or swap it.
