## February 2027 · My Laundry, My Job

**Header:** FAMILY MEETING CLUB · FEBRUARY 2027
**Title:** My Laundry, My Job.
**Subtitle:** Our things are our responsibility. Even small kids can do part of their own laundry.

**Letter** (about 140 words; for Megan to approve or rewrite):

> Laundry is one of those jobs that quietly lands on one person. It gets sorted, washed, folded and put away, and
> most of the family never sees how.
>
> This month, everyone takes a real part in their own clothes. Not all of it at once: the part that fits where
> they are. Your family's laundry plan is already prepared from your family page, with a picture guide for each
> child's level. Younger children start with "My Clothes, My Job". Older children work toward "I Can Do My Own
> Laundry".
>
> Pick your laundry days together and decide who runs the machine. Then let each child do their part.
>
> Here's to a little less laundry on one person.
>
> Warmly, *Megan*

**Back:**
1. **Start where they are.** Age suggests a starting level; you can change any child's level if their readiness is different.
2. **One guide per level.** Children at the same level share one printable.
3. **The machine stays with a grown-up** until they're trained and supervised as appropriate (the page names who runs it).
4. **Ask in this week's Family Meeting** (sage panel): *What's one part of your laundry you could do yourself this week?*
5. **Scan for your family's plan:** the existing QR code and `organizedmomcollective.com/club/kits/2027-02-better-together`.

## October 2027 · Less asking. More doing.

**Header:** FAMILY MEETING CLUB · OCTOBER 2027
**Title:** Less asking. More doing.
**Subtitle:** One familiar cue. A few picture jobs. A finish line kids can recognize.

**Letter** (about 140 words; for Megan to approve or rewrite):

> Mom shouldn't have to notice every mess, start every cleanup, and check every job.
>
> This month's Daily Reset is already prepared for your family. It gives your children one cue they'll recognize,
> like "before dinner", two or three picture jobs, and a clear finish line, so they can take responsibility for one
> space a little at a time.
>
> Start with the belongings your kids leave around the house. Practice the reset together once. Then let them
> check the pictures and finish on their own. Younger children may still need help to begin, and that's fine.
>
> The back of this sheet is for you: how to remind, how to help them start, and how to let them finish.
>
> Here's to less asking, and more doing.
>
> Warmly, *Megan*

**Back** (the page's own grown-up guidance, in its order):
1. **Remind them of their job:** use the cue, not a list of instructions.
2. **Help them begin if needed.**
3. **Let them finish on their own.**
4. **Let the routine remind them:** fewer reminders, over time.
5. **Ask in this week's Family Meeting** (sage panel): *How do we know our cleanup is finished?*
6. **Scan for your family's reset:** the existing QR code and `organizedmomcollective.com/club/kits/2027-10-home-works-together`.
