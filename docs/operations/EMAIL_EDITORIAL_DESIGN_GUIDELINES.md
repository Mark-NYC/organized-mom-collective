# OMC email editorial and visual standards
Updated November 10, 2026. Reference: actual Clean Mama marketing email screenshot provided by the owner, subject "Your home can feel easier and you don't have to be ready to start!" and the owner's prior Clean Mama inbox subject-line review.

## What the example actually demonstrates
The screenshot shows a long sales email for Clean Mama's Homekeeping Society annual offer. Near the top it explains the offer and membership value in regular paragraphs, with a single prominent filled CTA. It follows with alternating image-and-text product features, consistent whitespace and type, occasional bolded/key phrases, then another offer near the bottom. Different product/bonus sections are separated visually instead of being buried in one list. Annual-only items appear as distinct, named pieces with illustrations. The email anticipates reluctance ("don't have to be ready to start") and provides a concrete next step. The screenshot is a *sales email*, not a model for the length of a twice-weekly newsletter. Its discount/bonus stack is not an approved OMC offer.

**Do not claim** we know the competitor's open rates, click rates, conversion, design A/B results, or precise fulfillment economics. These are observations about visible content, not proven causes of performance.

## Apply now
Keep Sunday Family Reset and Wednesday One Small Win short, practical and largely editorial. Use a small amount of **bold / deep-slate emphasis** to surface a distinctive instruction or key benefit, never indiscriminately bold whole paragraphs. Prefer 1 concise highlighted fragment, optionally 2 for longer messages, and keep text legible in Outlook and Gmail.

When a real site destination is present, give its primary CTA a **consistent, recognizable visual treatment**: a generously padded, accessible deep slate (#3D6671) or warm ivory/soft frost button-like link. Do not invent links in messages that intentionally end with an action rather than a product pitch. Maintain surrounding whitespace. Include a short descriptive CTA label ("See the personalized family tools", "Make a free routine chart") rather than generic "Click here". One primary CTA per ordinary editorial email is enough. Full promotional emails can use two: one early and one after offer support.

Maintain intact UTM query parameters and any `#omc_pass={{{contact.RESOURCE_PASS}}}` fragment on resource links. Email markup must stay table-based, inline CSS, fallbacks for fonts, clear text-only alternative, the mailing address, and `{{{RESEND_UNSUBSCRIBE_URL}}}`. No images are necessary for every editorial tip. Don't add subject-line gimmicks or emoji everywhere.

## Hold for a future sales email build
A distinct **premium promotional layout** can have: a visible offer early, real photography/screenshots of the OMC mailed printables, clear product panels for the personalized monthly print / physical mailing / weekly family meeting, benefit-specific named examples, objection-handling copy, a primary CTA after the offer and again near the end. Use *real* printables and product images, not generated approximations claimed as actual products. Only promise bonuses and annual pricing after actual offer approval. Keep typography Georgia for headlines, Montserrat for body, and brand palette: Charcoal #545454, Warm Ivory #FBF7F1, Soft Frost Blue #CDDDE1, Leaf Green #D4DFC4, deep slate #3D6671.

## Subject-line reference
The owner's Oct–Nov 2026 inbox sample showed Clean Mama's recurring "Routine Reset" and "Quick Tip" conventions; specific situational subjects ("What I Do Before Guests Come Over"), conversational curiosity, and seasonal context. Keep our recurring *series* in the masthead; use the subject to signal a specific family situation or practical benefit. Don't directly copy competitor text.

## Scope and safe updates
Only modify unscheduled editable Resend newsletter drafts. Never cancel scheduled broadcasts simply to restyle them. Avoid changing email contents, destination URLs, segmentation, delivery times, opt-in, suppression, or Day 2/Day 6 welcome automation as part of a typography pass. For each edited draft, read back HTML/text to confirm unsub link, personalized passes, recipient segment, and CTA destination. When approved emails are sent, use verified performance rather than invented click uplift to refine the style.

## Applied to existing future Resend drafts, November 10, 2026

All **17 unscheduled draft newsletters** (One Small Win 04–12 and Sunday Family Reset 05–12) were edited in Resend using the existing raw HTML/plain-text content. Each draft has one carefully selected short key sentence or phrase emphasized using deep slate `#3D6671` and bold weight. Where a site URL existed, its existing editorial CTA was restyled into a clear padded, high-contrast deep slate link (`#3D6671` background, white copy), preserving the exact destination, existing UTM query and any `#omc_pass={{{contact.RESOURCE_PASS}}}` fragment. Emails without a link remain useful stand-alone messages rather than gaining irrelevant CTAs.

Formatting is restrained: one primary linked CTA per ordinary editorial email when possible, not multiple promotional blocks. The original subject line, sender, segment, scheduled date, actual editorial substance and the mandatory unsubscribe footer were not changed. The seven broadcasts already scheduled in Resend were intentionally left untouched because Resend forbids editing them without canceling the schedule.

The styling is based on the screenshot observation, not a claim of measured uplift. Check mobile Gmail and Outlook rendering on one test campaign before changing the templates for future sends. As future drafts are created, apply these same guidelines from the start.
