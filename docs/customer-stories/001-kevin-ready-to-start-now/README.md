## The story

A buyer received the new 2027 family wall calendar on November 9, 2026, and wrote immediately to say he loved the layout. Rather than wait until January to start using it, he asked whether November and December 2026 were available to purchase:

> “Hi just received it today, thank you!
>
> I was wondering if it's possible to order the current and next month? We love the layout and would like to get good use out of it now lol please let me know if it's possible and the cost ?”

The product did not have a preprinted 2026 edition. The founders decided to create two 11×17 planning pages and arrange local Staples pickup for the customer at no charge as a thank-you for being an early buyer. A Staples order confirmation was shared on November 9. **Do not state that the customer has picked up or used the additional prints until confirmed.**

## What we learned

1. **Immediate demand:** The buyer wanted to start using the calendar as soon as it arrived, even though the dated edition begins in January.
2. **Product validation:** “We love the layout” is spontaneous positive feedback from a paying customer.
3. **Willingness to pay:** He proactively asked what additional pages would cost.
4. **Repeated request:** A separate early buyer, Raquel (Customer Story 002), also asked for November and December 2026 pages.
5. **Service opportunity:** A thoughtful, low-cost local print solution may build customer goodwill.

## Product and marketing implications

- Test a free “Start Now” printable companion for buyers of future-dated calendars.
- Consider using the customer’s own phrase, “We love the layout,” with permission.
- Keep the offer separate from any request for an Etsy review. Never condition gifts on a positive review.
- Track cost and complexity of one-off print fulfillment before turning it into a standard offer.
