# Tony Hill-inspired Pinterest research and production loop

Owner: Organized Mom Collective. Established October 20, 2026. This is an **internal operating hypothesis**, not an endorsement from Tony Hill or a claim about guaranteed reach.

## Sources and boundaries
- Tony Hill, [Pinterest home-feed uniqueness](https://www.tonyhill.co/blog/pinterest-home-feed-uniqueness): differentiate visual ideas; avoid near-duplicate creative.
- Tony Hill, [Blue Pinterest strategy](https://www.tonyhill.co/blog/blue-pinterest-strategy): combine proven formats with distinctive experimentation.
- Tony Hill, [Pinterest strategy / Pin Drafting](https://tonyhill.co/blog/y2k-pinterest-strategy): inspect related interests and competitor pins; treat adjacency or drafting as a test, never a confirmed ranking trick.
- Hill's reported results are self-reports and do not establish expected results for OMC. Do not invent search volumes or competitor performance.

## Standing 10-pin research mix
Use **6 proven keyword ideas, 3 specific-audience/situation ideas, and 1 creative experiment per 10-pin batch** as our *proposed testing allocation*, not a rigid retrospective description of old assets. Existing master IDs, slot assignments, approved assets and prior production mappings remain authoritative. The master CSV's new "Research lane (proposed)" field is a forward-looking slot-based allocation (slots 1-6 / 7-9 / 10), not evidence a pin has been researched, created or approved. If an existing approved concept doesn't fit its proposed lane, preserve the creative and log the exception; do not redesign history to satisfy quotas.

## Before assigning next pin
1. Read `docs/editorial/pinterest/MASTER_TRACKER_README.md`, master CSV, reconciliation and current approved assets. Identify exactly ten available IDs. Never recycle an approved ID or treat planned publish dates as actual publication.
2. Research Pinterest search autocomplete, guided refinements, Trends (when available), boards, and relevant current top pins. Note date and observable evidence. Separate verified keyword terms from the CSV's **Pinterest keyword (hypothesis)** field.
3. Find 1-3 relevant competitor/reference pins per angle when practical. Record direct URL and what works (visual format, keyword, promise), **never** fabricated saves, clicks, views or virality. Do not copy the design or imply competitor pins are approved assets.
4. Find underserved situations: kid age, school stage, parent time constraint, family size, room, daily trigger, desired outcome. Only use sensitive categories such as ADHD when the linked resource addresses them accurately and respectfully.
5. Map each pin to one actual existing URL or an explicit content gap. The page must fulfill the visual/headline promise immediately. Choose intended next action: read guide, use free generator, subscribe when genuinely offered, explore responsibility cards when actually available, or calendar purchase when relevant. Never assert a checkout/email workflow exists without verifying.
6. Design a distinct composition, not a text-overlay swap. Keep the article and product truthful, preserve authorized assets and brand identity, and check for duplicates across approved pins.
7. Add evidence and an observed date to the master research fields; unknowns stay blank. Verify before claiming a keyword as "verified."
8. After publishing, compare impressions, saves, outbound clicks, and purchase/subscribe signals for the 6/3/1 lanes. Test and revise the split over time instead of assuming Hill's ideas guarantee reach.

## Required CSV fields
- `Research lane (proposed)`: proposed 6/3/1 bucket; doesn't override original creative job.
- `Target Pinterest keyword (verified)`: only when Pinterest evidence confirms the phrase; else empty.
- `Specific audience / situation`: exact household moment or person the pin addresses; else empty.
- `Competitor reference pin URL`: true direct URL from observed public Pinterest pin; else empty.
- `Visual differentiation / format`: one sentence explaining how image and composition differ from prior approved pins.
- `Intended conversion action`: verified, relevant next action on the actual landing page.
- `Pinterest research evidence / checked date`: source + date + what was observed, no invented metrics.

## Example: research-to-conversion journey
"Morning routine for kindergarteners" -> real routine article / generator -> optional signup where confirmed -> responsibility cards only if listed -> family calendar. A conceptual journey is not proof that an offer, integration or email sequence is live.

## Production QA
For every next batch show its ten master IDs, lanes, evidence, direct destinations, genuinely different image concepts and missing-content blockers before Gemini generation. For each pin, retain its original ID throughout Gemini, Claude import and final reconciliation. Separate **planned**, **created**, **approved**, **scheduled**, **published** and **performance** counts. Actual research and account data outrank the proposed split.


## Seasonal and conversion gate (2026-10-30)

Read [SEASONAL_ACQUISITION_PLAN.md](SEASONAL_ACQUISITION_PLAN.md) before each new 10-pin batch. For each candidate record the content lane (inspiration / useful / product), seasonal need month, Pinterest first-publication window (usually 60–90 days ahead of need), verified destination and next visitor action, relevant specific Club kit or calendar, and whether its enrollment deadline/delivery supports the claim. Preserve permanent master IDs and proposed versus actual schedule distinction; add seasonal context without overwriting existing approvals. Reconcile performance and decisions at 30/60/90 days rather than treating 500 created pins as success.
