Table stakes: check the week's schedule; a list of family favorites; leftovers; one shopping trip from a list; involve the family; a template.

Gaps and weak spots:

- **"Plan around your schedule" is said, not shown.** Nobody gives a simple way to sort the nights before choosing food.
- **Seven dinners planned for seven nights.** Plans assume every night gets cooked, so the first late practice breaks them.
- **Recipe-first.** Many results are menus or paid plans; the method is a side note.
- **No fallback** for a plan that blows up on Tuesday.

## Differentiation

- **The Shift:** a meal plan doesn't fail for lack of recipes; it fails because it plans dinners for nights you don't have. Sort the nights first, then choose the food.
- **Full / short / zero nights**: three labels, each with a matching kind of dinner, shown as a ruled list (`<Versions>`).
- **Plan four or five dinners, not seven.** Zero nights are part of the plan, not a failure of it.
- **A worked example week** where one full-night dinner feeds a zero night.
- **Swap, don't scrap** when the week changes.

## Structure

1. Tension (word for word): "It’s 5 pm. Someone asks what’s for dinner, and you open the fridge hoping it will decide for you. It doesn’t, so you decide. Again. Same as last night."
2. Short answer (word for word): "Here’s the short answer: **once a week, look at the week ahead, label each night by how much time you’ll actually have, plan four or five dinners to match, and write the grocery list from that plan.** It takes about 20 minutes."
3. The Shift: "Plan the nights before the food." — plans assume seven cooking nights; label the nights first.
4. Real-Life Version: "The 20-minute meal planning method": look at the week; label each night (full / short / zero, as `<Versions>`); plan four or five dinners; shop the kitchen first, then write the list; put the plan where everyone sees it.
5. Visual: `<Versions>` for the three kinds of night + a worked example week (table), one line on what to notice.
6. When Life Happens: plan blew up → swap, don't scrap; missed planning day → plan three nights; bad week → every night is a zero night, and that's still a plan.
7. Bridge: Thursday's Kitchen Reset fridge check (HC-01) is the natural moment to plan.
8. Product: calendar `<ProductCTA>` — meal plan and grocery list on the same weekly page as each day's schedule.
9. Next Small Win: label the next five nights full, short or zero. Labels only.
