## Differentiation

- **The Shift:** "The question isn't screen vs. paper. It's who updates the plan." A Skylight pays off when the changes arrive on adults' phones; paper pays off when the problem is that nobody looks.
- What we'll do that the top results don't: dated primary-source facts only; the same family week run through both; say plainly where the Skylight wins; no reviews, ratings or reliability claims either way.

## Structure (draft wording; finalize after the claim log is filled)

1. Tension: The practice time changed in a group text, and the paper calendar on the fridge still says 4:00. You've seen the Skylight ads. Is a screen on the wall the fix, or another thing to keep up with?
2. First H2 (the reader's question): **Is a Skylight Calendar worth it, or is a paper wall calendar enough?** Short answer, ≤100 words, by use case:
   - Skylight is the better buy if plans change several times a week, the adults already keep the schedule on their phones and want it on the wall without retyping, or nobody wants to hand-write.
   - A paper wall calendar is enough if the week is fairly steady, the main problem is that nobody looks, and you want meals, groceries and to-dos beside the schedule at no screen cost.
   - Keep what you have if it already works.
3. The Shift: as above, 30–60 words.
4. Comparison table (mobile, 3 columns): upfront cost · ongoing cost · how events get on it · last-minute changes · kids reading tomorrow · meals/lists beside the schedule · power/Wi-Fi · what happens if no one updates it. Only cells backed by the claim log.
5. Real-Life Version: "Run the same week both ways" (soccer moved by text, dentist, field trip slip), mirroring FP-11's scenario so the two read as a pair without repeating its table.
6. Switching: what carries over, what needs re-entering, test for two weeks with free tools first.
7. When Life Happens: the screen and phones disagree; the paper copy is stale by Wednesday; nobody checks either.
8. Product CTA (`calendar`): the 11 × 17 printed weekly wall calendar plus companion cleaning website; prices imported from `src/config.ts` (`CALENDAR_OPTIONS`), not retyped. State plainly it does **not** sync with or replace a digital calendar.
