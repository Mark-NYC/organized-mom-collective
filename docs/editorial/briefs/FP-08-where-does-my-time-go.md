Table stakes: track for a few days, no judgment, compare against what matters.

Gaps and weak spots: they all track **hours in blocks**. A mom's lost time isn't in the blocks; it's the repeats between them: the same question answered again, the same thing looked for, the same decision made again at 5:40. Hour logs also take effort she doesn't have. Nobody turns the findings into a fix the family can see.

## Differentiation

- **The Shift:** "Track the repeats, not the hours." A time audit that logs hours misses where the day goes; the leak is the small thing done twice. One answer written where everyone looks replaces ten askings.
- What we'll do that the top results don't: a three-day tally (marks, not minutes) in three columns, and a fix for each column that moves the answer out of her head and onto the wall, so the family can answer it themselves. Ends in a 30-minute command center reset, matching the Refresh Sheet.

## Structure

1. Tension (word for word): "It’s 9 pm and you can’t say where the day went. Nothing big happened. You answered “what time is practice?” three times, looked for the permission slip twice, and decided dinner at 5:40, again."
2. Short answer (word for word): "Here’s the short answer: **for three days, tally the small things you do twice: the questions you answer again, the things you look for, the decisions you make again. Then fix the top three by putting the answer where your family can see it.**"
3. The Shift: "Track the repeats, not the hours." (30–60 words)
4. Real-Life Version: "The three-day leak check"
   1. Put a card by the calendar with three columns: Asked again · Looked for · Decided again.
   2. Tally, don't time: one mark each time, nothing else.
   3. After three days, circle the three biggest.
   4. Give each a fix by column: asked → write the answer on the calendar; looked for → give it one home at the command center; decided again → decide it once in the weekly reset.
   5. Reset the command center (about 30 minutes): clear what's expired, write the must-keeps for the coming weeks, put the three fixes in place. Show the family where the answers live.
5. Visual: a three-row table, "Leak → Where the answer goes", one example per column (practice time → calendar entry with time and driver; permission slip → the needs-attention pocket; dinner → the week's meal plan).
6. Adapting it: one line inside the routine (kids old enough to read answer their own questions from the calendar).
7. When Life Happens: only one day of tally → fix the top one; the leak comes back → the answer isn't where they look, move it; a sick week → skip the check, run it next week.
8. Product note: `<ProductCTA>` calendar, after the routine. Verified feature only: the daily schedule column per day and the meal plan on one weekly page (ARTICLE_FORMAT.md → Product notes).
9. Next Small Win: "Tonight, stick a note beside your calendar with three words: asked, looked for, decided. Start the marks at breakfast."
