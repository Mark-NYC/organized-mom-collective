## Search intent

Primary query: *how to organize a family calendar*. Intent type: how-to. She wants one calendar the whole family checks, so she stops being the only place the schedule lives.

Secondary keywords (use naturally): family calendar, family wall calendar, what to put on a family calendar, family calendar ideas, color coding a family calendar, shared family calendar.

Related questions (result summaries and related searches): paper or app; where to put it; color-code by person or by category; what to include; how to get kids and a partner to use it; who updates it and when.

Table stakes: pick one format; a visible spot; color coding; include school, activities, appointments; involve the kids; agree who updates it.

Gaps and weak spots:

- **Tool-first.** Several top results sell a calendar display or app; the advice tilts toward features (sync, colors, categories).
- **No entry format.** "Include the details" is said, but no one shows a single line everyone writes the same way.
- **No "what to leave off".** Lists grow to chores, bills, every to-do; the calendar gets too full to read.
- **No weekly look.** Few say *when* anyone reads it, which is where calendars actually fail.
- **No fallback** for the week it falls behind.

## Differentiation

- **The Shift:** the calendar doesn't fail because of the format (paper vs app, colors). It fails because it's a record one person keeps, not a page the family reads. If anyone can read tomorrow without asking you, it works.
- **One entry formula** (time · what · who · what to bring), shown as a blockquote and in a real photo of filled-in days.
- **An on / off table**: what changes the day goes on; what never changes and long to-do lists stay off.
- **Colors optional**, one per person at most.
- **A five-minute look at the week**, with a pointer to the weekly reset (FP-04, later).
