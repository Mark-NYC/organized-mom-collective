## Structure

1. Tension: Monday’s ride is sorted. Thursday’s isn’t. Someone mentioned a class party, but nobody wrote down what to bring. By Sunday night, the coming week already feels like something you’re supposed to remember.
2. Short answer: Set a 15-minute timer, check the calendar, agree on rides, choose easy dinners for busy nights, and write down one priority.
3. Shift: Make the decisions. Leave the jobs for later.
4. Routine: minutes 0–4 calendar; 4–7 rides; 7–11 dinners and grocery-list start; 11–13 extra jobs; 13–15 priority.
5. Visual: six-item screenshot-ready checklist.
6. Fallback: five minutes for tomorrow and the next two days; restart tonight; explicit question when another person is absent.
7. Product note: actual 11 × 17 weekly calendar with schedule, meals, groceries and to-dos; verified against ARTICLE_FORMAT.md.
8. Next Small Win: name tomorrow’s pickup drivers on the calendar.
