# Brand voice

Who we write for, what we believe, and how it sounds. Read before writing or editing any article.

Related: [ARTICLE_FORMAT.md](ARTICLE_FORMAT.md) (structure) · [SEO_STRATEGY.md](SEO_STRATEGY.md) (search) · [ARTICLE_WORKFLOW.md](ARTICLE_WORKFLOW.md) (process).

## Audience

A mom running a busy household: school-age kids (or younger), activities, often a job, and most of the family's planning in her head. She reads on her phone, in a spare minute: in the pickup line, after bedtime, while dinner's in the oven. She arrived from Google with a specific problem ("weekly cleaning schedule", "what's for dinner this week") and limited patience.

What she has plenty of: advice, printables, Pinterest boards, apps. What she's short on: time, and room in her head for one more thing to keep track of.

What she wants from us: a clear answer, one routine she can start today, and permission to do less of it on a bad week without the whole thing falling apart.

## Positioning

**Organized Mom Collective helps busy moms run their home with simple systems that reduce the number of decisions they make.**

- Not aspirational organizing. We don't sell the matching-bins, perfect-pantry version of home life.
- Not productivity culture. The goal isn't to fit more in; it's to carry less in your head.
- Not a lecture. We don't tell her she's doing it wrong or that she should love it.

Our signature format, **The Real-Life Reset**, is how that shows up on the page: answer, insight, routine, fallback, one small win. See [ARTICLE_FORMAT.md](ARTICLE_FORMAT.md).

The products follow the same idea: a weekly paper wall calendar (schedule, meals, groceries, to-dos, prayer and gratitude on one page) and a companion app that turns each day's cleaning zone into a short checklist. Articles stand on their own; the products are one way to put the advice into practice. See "Product mentions" in [ARTICLE_FORMAT.md](ARTICLE_FORMAT.md#product-notes).

## Convictions

These are the ideas we return to. Every article should express at least one of them through its advice, not by stating it as a slogan.

1. **Fewer decisions beat more motivation.** A routine works when it answers "what do I do now?" before she has to ask. Systems over willpower.
2. **Plan for the missed day.** Real weeks include sick kids and late practices. A plan that only works in a perfect week isn't a plan. Every article has a fallback.
3. **Small and regular beats big and occasional.** Fifteen minutes a day keeps a house; a lost Saturday doesn't.
4. **If it only lives in your head, it isn't a system.** Write it down, put it where the family can see it, and let the calendar or list do the remembering.
5. **Running a home is shared work.** Systems should be clear enough that anyone in the family can pick up a task without asking her how.
6. **Stop on time.** A routine with an end is one you'll start again tomorrow. Finishing the timer matters more than finishing the list.
7. **Room for what matters.** The calendar makes space for prayer, gratitude and the people she loves. We mention this lightly and respectfully, never as a sermon.

## Tone

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Warm: we're on her side | Gushing, cheerleading, "you've got this, mama!" |
| Direct: the answer comes first | Coy, teasing, building suspense |
| Practical: specific steps, times, examples | Vague: "find what works for you" with nothing behind it |
| Calm: problems are normal and fixable | Alarmist, guilt-inducing |
| Plain-spoken: short words, short sentences | Corporate, clinical, or influencer-speak |
| Honest: realistic about time and effort | Promising transformation, "game-changers", "life hacks" |

Read the draft out loud. It should sound like a capable friend explaining what she does, not a brand talking about itself.

## Style rules

- Write to one reader: "you". The brand is "we" only when talking about the products.
- Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences on a phone screen). One idea per paragraph.
- Concrete beats abstract: "15 minutes after dinner" beats "a little time each day".
- Numbers are numerals with units: 15 minutes, 3:30, 11 × 17.
- Bold the one phrase in a section that she'd remember if she skimmed. Not more.
- Curly quotes and apostrophes (’ “ ”) in body copy, matching the existing site.
- Sentence case for headings, except product and zone names, which match the printed calendar exactly (Daily Reset, Living Room, Entry/Bathroom, Kitchen Reset, Deep Cleaning, Home Project, Catch-Up / Reset).
- American spelling.

## Never

- **Invented anything:** statistics, studies, expert quotes, testimonials, reviews, case studies, or "a mom in our community told us…".
- **Fabricated first-person experience:** no "as a mom of three", "in my house we…". The brand has no persona; it speaks as Organized Mom Collective.
- **Unsourced claims of fact.** If a claim needs a source (health, safety, food storage), link a reputable one or leave it out.
- **Guilt or shame:** "you should", "lazy", "no excuses", comparisons to other moms.
- **Excessive reassurance** that replaces advice: "it's okay, mama, you're doing amazing" with no next step.
- **Filler openers:** "In today's busy world…", "Let's face it…", "We've all been there…", "Are you tired of…?"
- **Hype words:** game-changer, life-changing, hack, ultimate, effortless, transform, secret, must-have.
- **Generic AI-shaped advice:** "consistency is key", "self-care is important", "find what works for you", lists of obvious tips with no system behind them.
- **Product features we haven't verified.** See [ARTICLE_FORMAT.md](ARTICLE_FORMAT.md#product-notes).

## Writing examples

### Openings

> ✗ In today's busy world, keeping a clean home can feel overwhelming. As moms, we wear so many hats! But don't worry: with the right cleaning schedule, you can have a spotless home without stress.

> ✓ You've printed a cleaning schedule before. It looked great on Monday. By Wednesday a kid was sick, Thursday's bathroom became Saturday's bathroom, and the schedule turned into a list of things you hadn't done.

Why: the second starts in her real week, names the actual failure, and doesn't promise "spotless".

### Giving the answer

> ✗ There are many ways to approach meal planning, and the best method depends on your family's unique needs. Let's explore some options!

> ✓ Here's the short version: once a week, look at the week ahead, label each night by how much time you'll have, plan four or five dinners to match, and write the grocery list from that plan.

### The insight

> ✗ The key to a successful family calendar is consistency and communication.

> ✓ Most family calendars don't fail because of the format. They fail because nobody looks at the week as a whole until it's already happening.

Why: the second challenges an assumption (the format is the problem) and points to a specific fix (the weekly look).

### Encouragement

> ✗ Remember, mama, you're doing an amazing job and your family is lucky to have you! 💕

> ✓ Missed a day? Skip it, don't stack it. Wednesday is still Wednesday's zone.

Why: real reassurance is a rule that makes the bad day easier.

### Product mention

> ✗ Our AMAZING calendar will transform your family's chaos into calm! Grab yours today before they sell out!

> ✓ On the Organized Mom Collective wall calendar, the week's meal plan and grocery list sit on the same page as each day's schedule, so Tuesday's practice is right next to Tuesday's dinner.

Why: one specific, verifiable feature, tied to the advice the reader just read.
