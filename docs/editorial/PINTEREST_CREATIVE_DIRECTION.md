# OMC Pinterest creative direction

## Authority and purpose

Read [PINTEREST_STRATEGY.md](PINTEREST_STRATEGY.md), [BRAND_VOICE.md](BRAND_VOICE.md), [../brand/VISION.md](../brand/VISION.md) and [../brand/PUBLIC-VISUAL-DESIGN.md](../brand/PUBLIC-VISUAL-DESIGN.md) at the start of each batch. They remain the source of truth for audience, promises, factual claims and brand identity. This document captures the owner-approved editorial direction and guides visual concept selection. It does not change audience, factual claims or product boundaries in the strategy.

OMC helps moms build organized families. Show children learning their part, caregivers sharing the teaching, and plans becoming visible. The story is shared responsibility, relief and growing capability. A chart supports practice; it does not automatically change family behavior.

**Make a useful, desirable piece of lifestyle editorial that earns attention before asking for a click.** A pin should have a specific reason to exist and a visual idea that supports it.

The early panel-led contact sheet and the six latest pin versions were visually inspected for this review. The complete prompts from separate earlier threads are not available here; their results and the owner's reported reactions are evidence, not grounds to invent missing production history.

## Creative standards

- One clear visual focal point and one main promise per pin.
- A specific, truthful headline with a reason to save or click.
- A composition chosen for the content: photograph, typographic reference, illustration or a combination.
- Meaningful variety in subject, scale, structure and contrast across the batch. Different colors or headlines alone are insufficient.
- Warmth through tangible materials and recognizable family life: oak, cotton, woven baskets, a school bag, dinner dishes and daylight.
- Space that creates hierarchy, not empty territory left over after fitting a template.
- Practical advice that survives a busy week. No perfection, guilt, exaggerated outcomes or fabricated customer stories.
- Fewer excellent pins are better than filling the requested count with weak or repetitive work. Record any shortfall.

Do not equate warm with beige. The batch used frost blue, apricot, blush, dark ink and natural wood. Return to the current brand palette for future work; photography can provide tonal richness without adding unrelated graphic colors.

## Photography and storytelling

Use an approved existing photograph when it carries the idea. Generate original supporting photography when a distinct scene is necessary and authorized. Never generate product artwork, a product screen, handwriting on a product or a fake customer endorsement.

No visible faces in generated people scenes. The owner rejected them because they read as AI. Use a complete back view, a natural crop below the face, or hands engaged in one clear action. This restriction must not create an apparently headless body.

Good action is concrete: a child puts a book into a backpack; an adult puts a cup beside the sink while the child carries a plate. An open hand hovering vaguely near a child is weaker and harder to make believable.

Prompt for camera position, subject placement, light, materials, the action and space needed by the eventual composition. Inspect what was generated; prompt instructions are not proof of correct anatomy or framing.

These are excerpts from prompts actually used, not universal templates:

> “Entire back of child's head INCLUDING all hair and natural neck is visible in middle of frame. No face, profile, reflection or facial features anywhere.”

This replaced a failed attempt to exclude heads, which produced an unnaturally cut-off child. Positioning the replacement photograph beneath the masthead kept the whole head visible.

> “Child ... holds ONE small cream ceramic dinner plate securely with two hands at opposite sides ... Adult ... his one visible hand sets a small cream cup DOWN on oak counter beside white sink.”

This replaced an ambiguous gesture with two connected, visible actions. The final deterministic crop excluded the small facial fragment at the top of the generated source.

> “REMOVE ONLY the blank white paper and its contact shadow; replace its occupied rectangle with matching uninterrupted natural oak tabletop grain.”

This was a supporting-background edit. The original chart was not an input to the image model. The generated wood repair was then used only within a masked region of the original scene.

## Typography and composition

Use type as an editorial tool: a confident sans-serif promise, a generous serif phrase, an italic emotional line or an oversized numeral can each establish a different reading rhythm. Keep at most two typeface families per pin. Preserve mobile readability.

The batch used Montserrat Variable and URW P052 Roman/Italic because Georgia was unavailable. That substitution is a historical fact, not a new default font policy. The strategy's Montserrat and licensed Georgia guidance remains authoritative. The batch also used darker ink, `#353b3d`, rather than the canonical `#545454`; retain this as a recorded batch choice, not a silent brand-token replacement.

Composition principles:

- Let typography and photography belong to the same idea. Copy need not always sit below a photo.
- Choose hierarchy before adding labels, rules or decoration.
- Make a shown product substantial enough to recognize. Keep its real artwork clear and unfaded.
- Use a solid copy ground when needed, but avoid repeating large translucent rectangles across the batch.
- Let margins and visible letter edges establish deliberate alignment. Do not rely on approximate coordinates.
- Give illustration a finished visual role. An unfinished doodle weakens otherwise polished typography.
- Keep branding discreet and readable. A large promotional block should not compete with the printable.

## Approval does not erase unresolved lessons

The latest fridge image still shows the magnet above the paper rather than visibly overlapping it. The record contains no verified correction. Keep the photographic/editorial concept, but do not copy that attachment detail into new work.

The search pins say “free chart” or “editable chart,” but do not explicitly say “generator.” The article and existing plan identify a free generator. Creative approval does not mean that distinction is adequately communicated. Future search-led tool pins must make the actual offer clear at feed size, within the existing truthful strategy.

No Pinterest performance data was collected. “Strong save candidate” and numerical self-ratings were editorial judgments, not measured results.

## Automate the mechanics, preserve the judgment

Automate asset hashes, uniform resizing, known layer placement, text bounds, dimensions, color profiles, filenames and packaging. Assist concept development with source excerpts and draft hooks.

Keep story selection, expressive hierarchy, photographic realism, anatomical review, physical integration, useful whitespace, feed appeal and final approval under visual judgment. A technically passing export is not automatically an approved creative.

Reusable code should expose independent layers and adjustable coordinates. It should not dictate six fixed compositions or force every article to reuse this batch's scenes, colors, three-job format or exact line breaks.
